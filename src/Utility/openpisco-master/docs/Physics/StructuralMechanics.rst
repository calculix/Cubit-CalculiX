####################
Structural Mechanics
####################

Structural mechanics analyses are used to predict the response of structures
subjected to mechanical loads and provide the physical quantities required by
many optimization criteria such as compliance, stress, eigenfrequencies, and
buckling loads.

Throughout this section, let :math:`\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3` denote the
computational domain and

.. math::

   \partial \Omega = \Gamma_D \cup \Gamma_N \cup \Gamma,

where:

* :math:`\Gamma_D` denotes Dirichlet boundaries.
* :math:`\Gamma_N` denotes Neumann boundaries.
* :math:`\Gamma` denotes traction-free boundaries.

Overview
========

OpenPisco provides interfaces to several structural solvers and supports
multiple mechanical analyses commonly encountered in topology optimization,
shape optimization, and structural design.

Supported Analyses
==================

* Linear Elasticity
* Static Buckling
* Modal Analysis
* Harmonic Analysis

Linear Elasticity
=================

The linear elasticity analysis predicts the displacement, strain, and stress
fields generated by external loads acting on an elastic structure.

Governing Equations
-------------------

The displacement field :math:`u` satisfies

.. math::

   \left\{
   \begin{aligned}
   \operatorname{div}(\sigma(u)) + f &= 0
      && \text{in } \Omega,\\
   \sigma(u)\cdot n &= g
      && \text{on } \Gamma_N,\\
   \sigma(u)\cdot n &= 0
      && \text{on } \Gamma,\\
   u &= 0
      && \text{on } \Gamma_D.
   \end{aligned}
   \right.

where

* :math:`u` is the displacement field,
* :math:`f` is the density of body forces,
* :math:`g` is the prescribed surface traction,
* :math:`n` is the outward unit normal vector.

Constitutive Law
----------------

Assuming small deformations and isotropic linear elasticity, the stress tensor
follows Hooke's law

.. math::

   \sigma(u)
   =
   2\mu e(u)
   +
   \lambda \operatorname{tr}(e(u))I,

where

.. math::

   e(u)
   =
   \frac{1}{2}
   \left(
   \nabla u + \nabla u^T
   \right)

is the infinitesimal strain tensor.

The Lamé coefficients are expressed in terms of the Young's modulus
:math:`E` and Poisson's ratio :math:`\nu` as

.. math::

   \lambda
   =
   \frac{\nu E}
   {(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)},

.. math::

   \mu
   =
   \frac{E}
   {2(1+\nu)}.

Static Buckling
===============

The static buckling analysis evaluates the stability of a structure subjected
to compressive loading and determines the critical load factor associated with
the onset of instability.

Governing Equations
-------------------

The analysis is performed in two stages.

First, the static equilibrium problem is solved to obtain the prestressed
configuration.

Then, the critical load factors are obtained by solving the generalized
eigenvalue problem

.. math::

   \left(
   K + \lambda K_G
   \right)u = 0,

where

* :math:`K` is the elastic stiffness matrix,
* :math:`K_G` is the geometric stiffness matrix,
* :math:`\lambda` is the critical load factor,
* :math:`u` is the buckling mode.

Modal Analysis
==============

Modal analysis computes the natural frequencies and vibration modes of a
structure.

Governing Equations
-------------------

The problem consists in finding the eigenpulsations
:math:`\omega_j > 0`
and associated eigenmodes :math:`r_j` such that

.. math::

   \left\{
   \begin{aligned}
   -\operatorname{div}(\sigma(r_j))
      &=
      \omega_j^2 \rho r_j
      && \text{in } \Omega,\\
   r_j
      &=
      0
      && \text{on } \Gamma_D,\\
   \sigma(r_j)\cdot n
      &=
      0
      && \text{on } \Gamma.
   \end{aligned}
   \right.

where

* :math:`\rho` is the material density,
* :math:`r_j` is the eigenmode associated with mode :math:`j`,
* :math:`\omega_j` is the corresponding eigenpulsation.

Harmonic Analysis
=================

Harmonic analysis predicts the steady-state response of a structure subjected
to periodic loading.

Governing Equations
-------------------

We consider the harmonic response problem of a visco-elastic body depicted by the following problem

.. math::

    \begin{equation}
		\label{u}
		\left\{ \begin{array}{rlll}
			\rho \ddot{u} + c\dot{u} - \text{div}(\sigma (u)) &= & 0 & \text{in }\Omega \times \mathbb{R}^{+}, \\
			u &= & 0 & \text{on }\Gamma_D\times \mathbb{R}^{+},\\
			\sigma(u) \cdot n &= &g(t) & \text{on }\Gamma_N\times \mathbb{R}^{+},\\
			\sigma(u) \cdot n &= &0 & \text{on }\Gamma_R \cup \Gamma_M \times\mathbb{R}^{+}.
		\end{array}
		\right.
	  \end{equation}

where:

* :math:`\sigma` is the stress tensor satisfying the Hooke's law
* :math:`\rho` is the material density
* :math:`c` is the damping operator
* :math:`g` is the density of surfaces forces acting on :math:`\Gamma_N`
* the body is clamped on :math:`\Gamma_D`
